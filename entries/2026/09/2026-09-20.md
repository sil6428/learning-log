# Practised cyber-crisis decisions and shipped Relay v0.2

## Session

- **Area:** Incident response, crisis coordination, and business continuity
- **Time spent:** Three-hour workshop and follow-up reflection
- **Goal:** Understand how an organization makes defensible decisions while a cyber incident is still developing and reliable information is limited.

## Work completed

- Attended the `Take the Helm: Living Through a Cyber Crisis` workshop at BSides Montreal 2026.
- Worked through a small-team scenario as the leadership of a mid-sized organization whose systems had gone down on a Saturday morning.
- Reviewed the supplied network diagram and backup information, then helped form an initial response plan before the full cause and scope were known.
- Responded to new information introduced during the exercise and considered technical containment alongside legal, press, executive, employee, and client communication.
- Took part in the closing debrief connecting the difficult moments in the exercise to preparation, documentation, tested backups, escalation, and established incident-response playbooks.

## What I learned

- Technical containment is only one part of a cyber crisis. Legal, communications, leadership, and operational decisions begin before the technical investigation is complete.
- Asset inventories, current documentation, clear ownership, and tested backups become decision-making tools during an incident rather than background administrative work.
- A team can become overloaded quickly when several workstreams require attention at once, so triage, delegation, and escalation need to be deliberate.
- Waiting for perfect information is not realistic. Decisions should record what is known, what is assumed, who owns the next action, and when the decision will be revisited.
- A tabletop exercise can reveal gaps in a response plan without requiring a real incident or intrusive technical activity.

## Problem encountered

- The scenario deliberately changed while the team was planning. The difficult part was keeping the response organized when technical facts, business pressure, and communication needs were all moving at different speeds.

## Next step

- Continue building technical investigation skills so future tabletop decisions can be supported by stronger network and system evidence.

## Project delivery

After the workshop reflection, I completed and published the next P2P Messaging milestone with Ghayas Sher's collaborative repository as the existing foundation.

- Replaced the development-only status page with a local browser workspace for first-time identity setup, device and history unlock, verified-contact conversations, authenticated replies, history search, drafts, pinned/muted/archived views, peer-card exchange, and live listener status.
- Kept the security boundary explicit: the web interface binds to the local computer, unlocked keys and sessions live only in process memory, authenticated mutations require a per-session CSRF token, and a new unlock invalidates the earlier browser session.
- Blocked sending until the contact's fingerprint has been marked verified and kept file support limited to a filename, size, and browser-calculated SHA-256 reference rather than claiming the file bytes are transferred.
- Added browser-flow and reply-reference tests, bringing the repository to 75 passing tests. Ruff, JavaScript syntax, diff, package-build, and visual checks also passed.
- Published the implementation and security documentation in commits `dc4f2ba` and `c1df0df`.
