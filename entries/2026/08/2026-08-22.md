## Second session

- **Area:** Three.js performance, adaptive rendering, geometry batching, reflections, shadows, testing, and deployment
- **Goal:** Reduce GPU and CPU load in the interactive portfolio without removing the room, changing its controls, or shortening the three-minute chess print.

## Performance work completed

- Added high and balanced rendering tiers based on pointer type, reduced-motion preference, device memory, CPU core count, screen size, and pixel density.
- Reduced the maximum render pixel ratio from 2 to 1.5 on capable computers and 1.1 on constrained devices.
- Reduced the live floor-reflection texture ceiling to 640 pixels, removed reflection multisampling, and limited reflection refreshes to 15 per second during camera movement, 8 during animated sequences, and 2 while idle.
- Limited the desktop shadow map to 1024 pixels and capped shadows at 96 meaningful room objects instead of assigning shadows to nearly every mesh.
- Excluded tiny details and the animated print layers from the shadow pass.
- Merged hundreds of chess-print layers into material and height batches while preserving the visible bottom-to-top print sequence.
- Added adaptive render pacing: up to 45 frames per second on capable computers, 30 on balanced devices, 18 or 12 while idle, and 2 while AFFAN_OS covers the room.
- Stopped Three.js scene rendering while the browser tab is hidden and resumed cleanly when it becomes visible.
- Added regression checks for the adaptive quality tier, batched print geometry, bounded shadows, reflection throttling, idle frame limiting, and hidden-tab behavior.

## What I learned

- A small download does not guarantee a fast 3D site. Draw calls, reflection passes, shadow casters, pixel density, and continuous animation often matter more after loading.
- Repeated geometry with the same material can be merged by print-height bands to keep an animation while lowering draw overhead.
- Reflection quality and update frequency can be controlled separately, which preserves the mirrored-room effect without rendering it at full speed every frame.
- Adaptive quality should respond to the visitor's hardware and display instead of treating every laptop and phone as the same device.
