## What I learned

- A configured tool is evidence of learning, but it is not automatically a project strong enough for a cybersecurity resume.
- A research reproduction becomes defensible when every claim maps to code, a recorded dataset source, a controlled environment, and generated evidence.
- Resume bullets should be the final summary of completed work, not the plan used to start it.
- Removing invalid rows is not a neutral cleanup step because the affected labels are unevenly distributed across BENIGN, DoS Hulk, PortScan, Bot, FTP-Patator, and DDoS.
- A close aggregate match does not prove full reproduction. The remaining per-class differences require lower-level flow construction and labeling evidence.
- Randomly splitting rows from a benchmark can place identical feature vectors and labels on both sides of the evaluation boundary.
- File holdout is a stricter test, but CICIDS2017 assigns every attack family to one source file. The resulting score measures unseen-class novelty rather than normal recognition of known classes.
- A 99% aggregate result can coexist with weak recall on rare classes. Macro metrics, per-class support, and split construction are required to interpret it.
